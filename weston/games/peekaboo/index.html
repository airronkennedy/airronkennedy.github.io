Why this game

Skill: object permanence, hide-and-find

Weston, peekaboo is already one of our favorite games, because you know I'm still here when I hide behind my hands — and it still makes you laugh when I pop back out. I want this iPad peekaboo to feel like that: we hide a friend under a blanket or a leaf, we look together, and they always come back. There is no trick and no “too late”; we just practice remembering that something can be there even when we can't see it, and we celebrate every reveal.

Research notes

  • ZERO TO THREE — Supporting Thinking Skills From 0–12 Months: peek-a-boo and simple hide-and-find games help a child learn object permanence — that things they can't see still exist — and build memory, which is the heart of this game.
  • ZERO TO THREE — Developing Thinking Skills from 12–24 Months: in the second year, toddlers pull the cover off a hidden toy, love repeating the same game, and learn with buttons that make something happen — so Weston does the reveal with one huge tap, and friends hide again and again.
  • Baillargeon, Spelke & Wasserman, 1985 — Object permanence in five-month-old infants: 5-month-olds already expect a hidden object to keep existing in its place, earlier than Piaget's 9-month search timeline — so this is joyful practice and agency (“I make you come back”), not teaching the idea from zero, and hides stay short.
  • CDC — Milestones by 2 Years: many 2-year-olds point to a picture when asked “Where is the bear?” and enjoy toys where a button makes something happen — the voice asks “Where's Teddy?” and a big blanket or leaf lifts when he taps it.
  • AAP — Media and Young Minds (2016): at this age, play together, choose high-quality apps, and avoid fast-paced or scary content — reveals are warm and predictable, never a jump-scare, and a grown-up is invited to sit and play too.
