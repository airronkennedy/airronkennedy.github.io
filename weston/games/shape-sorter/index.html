Why this game

Skills: circle · square · triangle · matching · hand control

I built a Shape Sorter because it's the classic toy for this age, and the CDC tells parents to do simple shape puzzles with toddlers and name each piece as it goes in, so the game says "circle," "square," or "triangle" every time he touches or fits one. I made the shapes different sizes, colors, and tilts, with tall and tipped triangles, because a study from Golinkoff and Hirsh-Pasek's team found kids talk about shapes more when they see unusual versions instead of only the perfect textbook ones. Dragging is harder than tapping for little hands, so he can drag a piece or just tap the hole he thinks it goes in, and a wrong hole just bumps, says what it is, and lights up the right one so he never fails.

How to play together

Say the shape names with him and trace the edges with your finger ("pointy corners, it's a triangle!"). The first round has one of each shape. Later rounds add a fourth piece and shuffle the holes. When the box is full, the shapes pop out and dance.

Research notes

  • CDC — Milestones by 2 Years: "Help your child do simple puzzles with shapes, colors, or animals. Name each piece when your child puts it in place." Every fit is named out loud.
  • Verdine et al. 2019, Early Childhood Research Quarterly — Effects of geometric toy design on parent–child interactions and spatial language: kids used more spatial language with sets that mixed standard and unusual shapes, and an app setting gave more shape names. So the pieces vary and the game names them. (I read the abstract via Europe PMC.)
  • ZERO TO THREE — Let's Play: toddlers are learning "in and out" and practicing matching. A sorter is in-and-out plus matching.
  • Bedford et al. 2016, Frontiers in Psychology: cites parent reports that toddlers tap (71%) far more than they drag (41%). That's why tapping a hole works as well as dragging.
  • AAP — Media and Young Minds: toddlers struggle to carry screen learning into the 3-D world, and parents re-teaching helps. Try a real shape sorter or cookie-cutter shapes after playing.
