Why this game

Skill: body words, pointing, co-play

I made this so Weston and I can play “Where’s your nose?” together, first on a friendly character on the screen and then on his own face and mine. I want him to hear the words, point, and get a little cheer, the same game we already play at bath time and in songs like Head, Shoulders, Knees, and Toes. This isn’t a test and he can’t get it wrong; it’s just a playful way to practice body words and notice his own body.

Research notes

  • CDC — Milestones by 2 Years: By 2, many children can point to at least two body parts when you name them, and a parent tip is to sing “Head, Shoulders, Knees, and Toes.” Prompts start with everyday face words, then move to a grown-up-started song.
  • Waugh & Brownell (2015) — Body-part words: At 20 and 30 months, children understood more body-part words than they could say, and early words clustered around eyes, nose, mouth, hair, and ears. Those five come first here; a wrong tap is named and the prompt is repeated, not scored as a miss.
  • AAP — Media and Young Minds (2016): Toddlers learn from screens best with a parent beside them, and they often need help connecting a screen to the real world. After finding a part on the friend, “On you” asks you to look at Weston and confirm together — no camera, no photo.
  • HealthyChildren — Watch Together: Shared, talked-about media is the useful kind. The song waits for a tap to start and does not loop on its own, so it is easier to stop and keep playing in the room.
  • ZERO TO THREE — Play Activities for 24 to 36 Months: Everyday body games (“Where’s your nose?”) belong in bath time and songs, not as a quiz. This page is a short co-play prompt, with cheers and stickers after a small handful of finds.
