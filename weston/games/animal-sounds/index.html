Why this game

Skills: animal words · listening · "Where is the…?" pointing

I chose Animal Sounds because Weston is right at the age of learning lots of new words, and the CDC lists pointing to things when you ask, like "Where is the bear?", as one of the big 2-year milestones. The game plays the sound first so he has to really listen, then says the animal's name clearly and waits for him to touch that exact animal, because studies found toddlers learn words from a touchscreen best when they have to tap the thing being named. If he taps a different animal it just tells him who that is, so there's never a wrong answer, and I made the big celebration wait until after the word is said, since research showed flashy animations right after the word can crowd out the learning.

How to play together

First the animals come out of the barn and say hello one at a time. Then the game asks "Who says moo?", and you can make the sound with him. The 👂 bubble repeats the question. Three finds start an animal dance party, and the next group comes out of the barn: two animals at first, then three. There are 8 animals in all.

Research notes

  • CDC — Milestones by 2 Years: most 2-year-olds "point to things in a book when you ask, like 'Where is the bear?'" The CDC also suggests helping kids "learn how words sound." So the game asks "Find the cow!" and repeats every name.
  • Kirkorian, Choi & Pempek 2016, Child Development — Toddlers' word learning from contingent and noncontingent video on touch screens: the youngest toddlers (24–36 months) learned a word from a touchscreen only when they had to touch the specific labeled object, not when any touch worked. So he taps the named animal itself. (I read the abstract via Europe PMC.)
  • Strouse & Ganea 2025, Journal of Experimental Child Psychology — Animated reinforcements disrupt young children's word learning: 19–23-month-olds learned more words with spoken praise ("good job!") than with a sound-and-animation reward. So spoken praise and the word come first, and the sparkles come after. (I read the abstract via Europe PMC.)
  • AAP — Media and Young Minds: at 24 months, children can learn words "from an interactive touchscreen interface that scaffolds the child to choose the relevant answers." It also says media at this age should be used together with a parent.
  • Hirsh-Pasek et al. 2015 — Putting education in "educational" apps: good apps are active, engaged, meaningful, and social. That's why the game uses familiar farm animals and sounds we can make together.
